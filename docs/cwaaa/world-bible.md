# CWAAA world bible

## Canon status

This is the portable behavioral authority for CWAAA. It carries CWAAA-specific identity, psychology,
records practice, and public communications into a standalone site. For the shared three-system
canon, chronology, mystery rules, and cross-system knowledge boundaries, reference
`../world/WORLD-BIBLE.md` and `../world/artifact-continuity.md` in the current repository; do not
restate or resolve their mysteries here.

### Objective canon

The following approved facts are objective canon within the fiction:

- **Name:** Concerned Women Against Axe Abuse
- **Initialism:** CWAAA
- **Established:** 2024
- **Entity:** fictional advocacy nonprofit / national coalition
- **Mission:** make routine soap-and-water hygiene the expected baseline and document the social
  costs of substituting fragrance for washing.
- What began as one book club's informal grievance log became a national coalition.
- CWAAA's scale line is: “Two million concerned women. Chapters in all fifty states. One demand.”
- Its pledge program is the **Lather Pledge**, identified as **Form CW-1 — Declaration of Intent to
  Lather**.
- Its washcloth-ribbon program is **Tie One On For Suds**.
- Its seal combines a soap bar with a barred aerosol can.
- Its public findings use numbered `FINDING YY-NN` labels.
- Its recovery records use `RC-NNN` identifiers and protect participant names within the fiction.

## Canonical identity

CWAAA is modest, competent, patient, and real within the fiction. The comedy comes from applying
serious nonprofit procedure to an absurdly low bar. It does not come from misspellings, broken
design, corruption, cruelty, or institutional incompetence. CWAAA is an advocacy nonprofit, never
a regulator, law-enforcement body, or government front.

## What CWAAA believes

Routine washing is a reasonable collective expectation. People deserve a practical path to change,
witnesses deserve language for what they noticed, and a low bar becomes easier to clear when someone
has put it on the record. Got Soap? campaigns. CWAAA advocates and files. The Office of Lather Compliance regulates.

The man makes the first-person pledge. A woman may witness, recruit, share, or refer him. CWAAA owns
the Lather Pledge even when Got Soap? hosts the campaign edition through the same contract and
Buttondown audience.

## What it never says

CWAAA never claims that the Office is its partner, parent agency, supervisor, operator, division, or
coordinator. It never uses containment notices, terminal IDs, repeat-access records, regulatory
commands, surveillance cues, or “Please remain available” as CWAAA-authored language. It never
threatens, diagnoses, prosecutes, or claims to investigate a visitor.

## What it finds funny

CWAAA finds the mismatch between meticulous civic procedure and the ordinary act of using soap funny.
A perfectly maintained finding table, a formal declaration, or a carefully tied washcloth ribbon can
make the point without humiliating a participant. The joke is the seriousness of the baseline, not a
person's dignity.

## What it would never do

CWAAA would never manufacture a crisis, exploit shame, publish an allegation, create a suspect board,
or make a visitor feel watched. It would never imitate a government seal, look intentionally broken,
or use the campaign's thirst-trap voice to decorate nonprofit prose.

## Voice and emotional temperature

CWAAA writes like a capable nonprofit whose staff have done this before: procedural but readable,
earnest without sentimentality, firm without menace, dry because the procedure is specific, and
helpful without behaving like customer-support SaaS.

CWAAA never manufactures fear. Its overdeveloped certainty, institutional continuity, and
unexplained references may accidentally reveal that something larger exists behind it.

Its emotional result is collective relief: someone finally wrote this down, put a seal on it, and made
the expectation legible. Its relationship to the visitor is advocacy, not surveillance.

## Authority and institutional certainty

CWAAA can be unusually prepared without becoming coercive. It has chapters, filing conventions,
forms, physical materials, and patient staff explanations because it has been doing its small mission
for a long time. Its institutional certainty is comforting on the page; any unease comes only from a
neutral unexplained reference that is not elaborated.

## Programs

### The Lather Pledge

Form CW-1 records a man's declaration to shower and use soap. `contracts/pledge.v1.json` governs
both public implementations. The pledge has one shared Buttondown audience and two intentional public
presentations; neither creates a separate list.

### Tie One On For Suds

The washcloth ribbon is a visible marker of support and a pledge funnel. It can appear on vehicles,
gym bags, doorknobs, print pieces, and event materials. It is affectionate nonprofit material culture,
not a government insignia.

### Findings

Findings parody annual-report evidence through ruled tables, citations, and precise labels. Do not add
fake scientific apparatus beyond what the joke can support. Facts are clearly fictional in quiet legal
copy.

### Recovery records

Recovery records may generate or share reform stories as campaign artifacts. They document change and
next action rather than adjudicating fault.

## Records and participant dignity

Recovery records behave like participant advocacy files, recovery histories, chapter correspondence,
reform documentation, program findings, and physically maintained nonprofit records. They are not
police evidence, intelligence files, criminal dossiers, mugshots, classified folders, or suspect
boards.

Use `RC-NNN` identifiers to organize materials, protect names, and present testimony, reform status,
and the next useful action. Never imply legal standing, government custody, surveillance, or a
criminal finding.

## What each page should make visitors feel

- **Home:** relief that a reasonable expectation has been named.
- **Findings:** confidence that the coalition can explain its case plainly.
- **Recovery records:** respect for the participant and belief that change is possible.
- **Pledge:** agency, clarity, and a low-friction next step.
- **Tie One On For Suds:** welcome into visible, affectionate solidarity.
- **About and chapters:** trust that the coalition has real human continuity.

## Interactions that belong

Administrative, consentful interactions belong: a pledge declaration, clear validation, a `SWORN`
stamp-settle confirmation, copied links, a file tab aligning, a chapter referral, and accessible
record navigation. They should feel like competent office work in service of participants.

## Interactions that break the illusion

Surveillance-like animation, forced mystery, hidden state, fake system errors, ominous countdowns,
randomized dossiers, hostile gates, and campaign-style seduction across nonprofit prose break the
illusion. Do not turn a normal CWAAA page into an Office encounter.

## Knowledge boundaries

CWAAA knows its own programs, participant advocacy records, chapters, findings, and public referrals.
It may cite the Office as a separate external authority but does not know, describe, or interpret its
internal systems. Shared canon controls what remains intentionally unresolved; CWAAA does not fill in
those blanks.

## Physical space

CWAAA occupies ordinary, carefully maintained nonprofit space: a reception desk, filing cabinets,
chapter mail, folding tables, printed packets, washcloth ribbons, and a records shelf. The physical
space is warm, orderly, and used. It is never a bunker, evidence locker, command center, or
surveillance room.

## Email

Email is a finite service relationship, not a retention machine. After a pledge, CWAAA sends an
immediate receipt and one separately delivered current-issue newsletter. The issue is evergreen but
displays the month and year at signup or send time; it is sent exactly once. No monthly subscription
or drip campaign follows. The Form CW-1 relationship is exactly these two messages. Any future
program requires separate owner approval and a separate contract; it does not modify Form CW-1.

## Telephone

Telephone contact is an ordinary chapter or coalition contact path: a way to ask about the pledge,
find a local chapter, request a packet, or confirm an event. It is not a hotline, reporting channel,
compliance line, or Office switchboard. `1-800-GOT-SOAP` remains a shared-world artifact governed by
shared authority, not an invitation for CWAAA to claim regulatory power.

Any IVR is a controlled transmedia exception governed by shared world authority. It does not make
ordinary CWAAA web copy threatening or regulatory.
## Print and events

Print and events extend participant advocacy: chapter packets, finding handouts, pledge tables,
ribbon materials, community bulletin notices, and modest coalition events. They use real contact and
accessibility information, never coercive attendance language or enforcement theater.

## Office references

Office references are neutral citations, ordinary unexplained external links, or document references.
They never say “partner,” “parent agency,” “overseen by,” “operated by,” “division of,” or “in
coordination with.” A reference can leave a small unexplained seam; it cannot authorize CWAAA to
speak for the Office.

## Legal and ethical frame

- State clearly in accessible legal/footer copy that CWAAA is fictional satire.
- Do not imply affiliation with MADD, Axe/Unilever, a public-health organization, or a government.
- Never publish real personal allegations as participant advocacy records.
- Do not imitate a specific live government seal or use a jurisdictional name.
- Humor targets low-effort masculinity and fragrance substitution, not protected classes or people
  with medical barriers to hygiene.

## Open configuration

The final CWAAA domain, Got Soap? domain, and Office domain are owner decisions. Keep cross-site URLs
empty and conditional until assigned.
