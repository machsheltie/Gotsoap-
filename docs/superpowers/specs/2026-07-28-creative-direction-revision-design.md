## Purpose

This revision prevents implementation agents from translating the three fictional systems into familiar website templates.

The governing author law is:

- **Got Soap? seduces.**
- **CWAAA validates, organizes, and makes its case in public.**
- **The Office of Lather Compliance assumes jurisdiction and refuses access.**

The three systems must feel authored by different institutions with different motives. Shared colors, cards, paper, or “official” styling do not create a world. A polished result still fails if it resembles SaaS, a generic nonprofit theme, a filing-cabinet website, Shopify, a fake-government terminal, or a portfolio template.

## 1. CWAAA creative platform

### 1.1 Thesis

**CWAAA: A Coalition Making Its Case in Public.**

CWAAA is a functioning national advocacy coalition presented through a dramatic public-advocacy exhibition system. It has a clear mission, credible programs, chapters, findings, recovery stories, a pledge, participant dignity, consistent institutional behavior, and understandable actions.

It also has an unusually strong creative department and enough confidence to present its case with force.

CWAAA is not:

- a document pretending to be a website;
- a filing cabinet, records-room simulation, government portal, or museum exhibit;
- decorative nonprofit theater made from beige paper, stamps, and form numbers;
- a generic nonprofit WordPress theme;
- a less-sexy Got Soap?;
- a fake regulator.

### 1.2 Two simultaneous layers

The **institutional layer** creates trust:

- ordinary, understandable navigation;
- mission clarity;
- clear program ownership;
- accessible forms;
- participant stories;
- chapter information;
- findings;
- contact and participation paths;
- functional page hierarchy;
- restrained institutional language.

The **expressive layer** prevents generic design:

- monumental public statements;
- exhibition-scale findings;
- intimate testimony;
- ~~documentary human traces~~ **documentary photographs of people** (struck 2026-09-15);
- tactile program artifacts;
- highly authored composition;
- route-specific spatial laws;
- narrative material transitions;
- sparse, cumulative wrongness.

The website is CWAAA’s public advocacy platform, not a literal tour of its headquarters. Physical operation is evidenced through correspondence, event materials, ribbon packets, signed declarations, testimony, reports, meeting notices, envelopes, and complete program artifacts that make sense beyond the visible crop.

### 1.3 Visitor progression

CWAAA earns trust before introducing unease:

1. **Home:** credible, sharp, funny, operational. No horror, Office breadcrumb, or performed mystery.
2. **Findings:** CWAAA has studied the problem far more seriously than expected. One sparse citation may appear.
3. **Recovery Stories:** record continuity is deeper than expected, but the files remain humane and participant-centered.
4. **Tie One On For Suds:** warmth, solidarity, and tactile relief.
5. **Pledge:** a safe, formal, meaningful civic commitment. No Office presence.
6. **Chapters and About:** legitimacy, scale, continuity, then a chronology crack.
7. **Office referral:** an administratively ordinary link creates a quiet rupture. The destination supplies the meaning.

No route may perform horror. Most routes need no fracture; no route may use more than one meaningful fracture.

### 1.6 Route choreography

Every route defines:

1. what owns the viewport;
2. what interrupts it;
3. its material transition;
4. the default layout it refuses;
5. the emotional change;
6. the fictional author.

#### Findings

Findings are monumental, public, collective, vertical, declarative, high-contrast, and exposed. The finding owns the viewport through an oversized figure, hard rule, short conclusion, supporting note, and disposition.

Meaningful interruptions may include a chapter annotation, methodology note, clipped citation, correction, or human observation. Findings are not dashboards, equal cards, widgets, or generic annual-report blocks.

At most a sparse neutral citation may read:

```text
RELATED AUTHORITY
Office of Lather Compliance
Establishment Directive 1961-A
```

It never glows, repeats on every finding, or explains itself.

#### Recovery Stories

Recovery Stories are intimate, individual, quiet, reflective, humane, and reading-oriented. One participant story owns the viewport through a restrained portrait/environment, personal object, testimony fragment, recovery identifier, and next action.

They are not police files, evidence boards, mugshots, classified records, suspect profiles, red-string walls, or case-card grids. Findings prove the issue; Recovery Stories show change.

#### Tie One On For Suds

Actual red cloth owns the route at human or macro scale: fibers, hem, stitch, fold, wear, tension, knot. Program information appears only on plausible objects such as a sewn label, hang tag, instruction slip, packaging band, or chapter distribution card.

The transition from Recovery Stories is:

> index/tab logic → cloth strip → tied symbol

Use real physical contexts: gym bag, doorknob, mirror, chapter table, or vehicle handle. No cursor-following ribbon and no simulated craft table.

#### Pledge

Form CW-1 is a singular ceremonial civic act. The declaration owns the viewport. It uses one accessible signing surface, persistent labels, adjacent errors, explicit two-message consent, and only artifacts with a real document relationship.

Success provides one `SWORN` settle, share, copy-link, exact fulfillment explanation, and ordinary CWAAA continuation.

No Office reference, transmission language, review threat, or surveillance joke may appear.

#### Chapters

Chapters use a composed field of human dispatches, correspondence, event images, ribbon packets, envelopes, meeting notices, regional environments, and chapter tables. The route is neither a card grid nor an animated map.

#### About

## 2. Office of Lather Compliance

### 2.1 Binding visual law

**THE OFFICE PAGE MUST APPEAR UNDER-DESIGNED.**

The Office website is a simple error page. It is not a designed terminal, government portal, horror UI, legacy-system concept piece, centered console, or stylish denial-state product.

Use:

- white background;
- black text;
- Courier New or direct system monospace;
- left alignment;
- ordinary harsh margins;
- plain line spacing;
- no logo;
- no navigation;
- no decorative frame;
- no status chip;
- no second typeface;
- no accent color;
- no shadow;
- no texture;
- no scanlines;
- no animation;
- no loading performance;
- no visible design flourish.

Approximate implementation:

```css
body {
  margin: 48px 24px 80px 52px;
  max-width: 760px;
  background: #fff;
  color: #111;
  font: 16px/1.45 "Courier New", Courier, monospace;
}
```

Margins reduce on mobile. The page should feel as if nobody believed it required art direction because the procedure itself was sufficient.

### 2.2 Preserved behavior

The approved copy, browser-local recognition, standing reference `8804-X`, terminal ID, three-session progression, storage/privacy constraints, no-flash requirement, and stubborn Continued Interest state remain governed by the Office PRD and visit-state contract.

## 3. Got Soap? Shop

### 3.1 Platform

**Official Campaign Supply: a fashion catalogue pretending to be a store.**

Shop behaves like a 1990s–2000s fashion campaign, luxury men’s grooming launch, fragrance advertisement, lookbook, and editorial merchandise catalogue. It does not behave like Shopify.

### 3.2 Index choreography

The Shop index refuses an equal product grid. Each product gets an oversized editorial performance:

- **The Embossed Bar:** cream soap, wet stone, lather, embossed mark — ~~macro porcelain still life~~ **STRUCK 2026-09-15: no still life; the bar is in a body's hands, in use.**
- **The Wordmark Tee:** male torso, dressing room, or locker-room editorial; identity rather than inventory.
- **The Statement Tee:** chrome or amber register; the statement reads at first-date distance.
- **The Dad Hat:** controlled candid or casting moment; attitude rather than SKU.
- **The Effort Bottle:** gym-to-shower transition; sweat is earned and lingering musk is retired.

For each product:

- image owns the viewport;
- name is oversized;
- one short blurb performs the sell;
- price reads as a fashion-caption detail;
- unavailable purchase control appears only where purchase normally occurs;
- movement resembles a lookbook, not inventory browsing.

A narrow **SUPPLY INDEX** may provide direct access without turning products into cards.

### 3.3 Unavailable checkout

`Coming Soon!` is a small intentional interruption, not a giant banner or broken state.

Example:

```text

```

The unavailable stamp/cross may appear after attempted purchase.

### 3.4 Product detail

Use one full-bleed hero, one severe crop, one materials/specification block, one price, one unavailable purchase control, and previous/next supply navigation.

Do not use thumbnail carousels, recommendations, ratings, accordions, product tabs, “customers also bought,” or generic ecommerce shells.
