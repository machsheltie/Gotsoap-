## Why each call went the way it did

**A.** Dylan and Priya both put (a) first for the same reason, arrived at independently. Dylan: "a
sentence *about me* that never says me… I immediately wondered whether I'm someone's one." Priya
graded it A and said it is "the only version that puts a woman and a man in the first sentence."
Maya ranked it second and still wanted it over the pick. The pick — the apparatus list — was last
for Maya ("props, not people… I'm being introduced to an organization by its office supplies") and
last for Dylan ("a list of features before I know why I'd want them"). Priya flagged (b)'s
not-X-but-Y couplet as an AI tell: "one of the model's four or five favourite rhetorical shapes, and
it always arrives in exactly this metre." Maya loved (b) and Dylan put it second; it loses to (a) on
Priya's tell plus Dylan's note that "smell" as an abstraction "lets me off," where (a) has a person
in it. **(a) also pays a structural dividend both Maya and Dylan named: it finally rhymes with
`close.traces[2]`, "Every member brought her own."**

**B — the raised-voice test is answered: no.** Priya's standing note from R1 ("the site never once
gets mad… one line that breaks it would tell me the writer has range instead of a setting") was
tested once, as proposed, with "We say it in public and we keep saying it. Wash. We are done phrasing
it gently." It was Maya's first choice. It failed with the two readers who matter for this line.
Dylan named the exact bail: "**'We are done phrasing it gently' makes me defensive.** It tells me a
previous, gentler version of this was tried on someone like me and failed, so we've escalated. It
puts me in the category of person you stop being nice to." Priya graded it C and flagged it twice:
"the model announcing the tone instead of having it. Anything that tells me it's no longer being
polite is being polite," plus the one-word mic drop that "is immediately followed by an explanation
of itself." All three liked the bare imperative "Wash." in isolation. It is the sentence after it
that does the damage.

Priya was the one reader who ranked the R3 pick first (A−), on a real craft argument: "put a number
on it" ties Advocate to the Findings apparatus, and "out loud" is "doing three jobs." All three
readers independently praised "out loud," and Dylan asked that "So far no one has argued with it out
loud" survive whatever happens to the clause. (b) keeps "out loud" and adds the cost Priya wanted —
"with her name attached… it costs her something" — while dropping the consultant-speak Dylan and
Maya both skimmed ("put a number on it," "state the expectation").

**Filed for a later lane, not acted on:** Priya's answer to her own standing note is that the crack
does not belong in a mission clause at all — "give me one moment where the institution's composure
fails — a struck-through line, a member statement that gets one sentence too honest and is followed
by '[remainder of statement not entered]'." That is a records-surface move, not a homepage move.

**Also filed, unread:** Dylan proposed a composite — "Someone has to say it out loud, with her name
attached. So far no one has argued back." No reader has graded it. It is an R4 candidate, not a call.

**C.** Unanimous. "How long the ride was" was the single most praised clause in the whole grading
set. Maya: "a gut punch… the only version that puts *duration* on the experience, which is the actual
injury." Dylan: "funny and horrible at once because it means someone was *timing it*." Priya: "the
kind of specific that can't be generated from a brief." All three rejected (a) for restating Finding
26-01 a screen above where the finding appears; Priya called out "asked to do X's job" as a
clever-balanced construction.

**D.** The R3 pick survives, and the readers reversed the proposal's own reasoning. Dylan pushed back
hardest of anywhere in the exercise: "That block builds to something and cutting the last line leaves
it as three notes about office furniture. This is the one place I'd push back hardest." Priya: "top
three lines on the site… the only place the satire admits this is personal rather than civic," and
graded the drop-it option a D. Maya preferred (a) "answer with a room" — genuinely the strongest
single alternate line in the set, per both women — but Priya placed it precisely: "'brought her own'
has a man in it; 'a room' has furniture," and it clones the *Most of them* opener already used in
Organize. **Priya's redirect, worth keeping:** use "Most of them answer with a room" as the opening
line of the Chapters page, "where 'a room' would have space to mean something." That is a CW-D04
item.

Both women flagged the three-trace stack as a rhythm problem rather than a line problem. Maya: "all
three are the same move: institutional noun, then a human deflation. By the third one I'm watching
the technique." Priya counted it as the fourth consecutive "withheld-noun button" and recommended
cutting the *middle* trace (the pledge table) rather than the last. Dylan and Maya both like the
pledge table ("I can picture the table"), so it stays 2–1; the stack's sameness is a live note for
R4, not a call here.

**E.** (b) "Nothing is filed at this address." Maya: "'nothing is filed' gets quietly menacing in a
way 'not in the public file' doesn't… 'at this address' is funnier and colder than 'page,' because
addresses are where people live." Priya: "*filed* is the active verb the whole site runs on, and
*address* does double duty — URL and building, which matters on a site whose office is above a
pharmacy. It stops sounding like a web page and starts sounding like a clerk." Dylan ranked the pick
first for its public/private implication, and put (b) second, keeping "a little of the bureaucratic
dread." (a) was unanimously last, and both Priya and Dylan gave the same reason: it breaks character
to apologise in the first person. Priya: "This organization does not say 'we do not have.' It says
'not entered.'"

**Jurisdiction check on (b):** "Nothing is filed" is a statement about the coalition's own records,
which is CWAAA's job. It is not a finding, a disposition, or a compliance status, and it carries no
error code, no containment language, and no repeat-access behaviour. The 404 stays calm and stays
CWAAA's. Maya's "quietly menacing" read is the acceptable edge of dry, not surveillance framing.

## Convergence outside the brief

These are not R3 calls. They are what three readers hit independently, and two of them are about
lines this lane was not allowed to touch.

1. **The APPROVED subline is the weakest sentence on the homepage — all three readers, unprompted.**
   `about.body[0]`, "Routine washing is a reasonable collective expectation," is APPROVED under
   design.md §1 and was not a lever in this lane. Maya: "Correct, load-bearing, and dull. It reads
   like the sentence a lawyer approved." Dylan: "Skipped it entirely on first pass. Pure policy
   voice. Zero image," and — the sharpest version — "'Reasonable' implies I'm being unreasonable, and
   I've heard that word in performance reviews. I skim it rather than fight it, which is worse than
   fighting it." Priya: "Read it twice, held nothing… pure mission-statement filler," and
   "the weakest sentence on the homepage." Both Dylan and Priya independently recommended that the
   R3 (C) winner **replace** it rather than join it. Priya's shape for the paragraph: "coalition +
   objects, then (b), then the elevators/rideshares/third row. All nouns, no positions."
   **Owner decision. The line is locked authority and nothing has been changed.**
2. **Four Recovery Stories records were dead ends, and all three readers stopped clicking.** Maya:
   "the index page is the best page on the site and clicking anything on it is a 60% chance of
   nothing," and "the four empty records did more damage to my trust than any line on the site."
   Dylan: "two dead clicks and out." Priya: "worse than a bad page — it retroactively makes the good
   pages feel like a demo," and it was the top item in her *what's missing*. Addressed in the same
   pass: see `recovery-stories-open-records.md` decision note.
3. **Finding 26-01 is read twice in full.** Priya: the home carries the statement, the 73% support,
   the entered-by, the disposition, the status and the methodology note, and then "/findings' first
   screen taught me nothing new." She also flagged the scale line appearing as both the utility bar
   on every page and the body of the home's own close: "once as chrome and once as content, makes it
   feel like chrome both times. Kill one." Home/Findings overlap item for CW-D03.
4. **The withheld-noun cadence is becoming a tic.** Priya listed six in reading order — "and one
   form," "four women and a binder," "the woman who shares the bed," "Some are load-bearing," "The
   tenth asked to leave the study," "Every member brought her own" — and Maya independently reported
   watching the technique by the third trace. Every one of those lines tests well alone. The note is
   about density, and it is the first real ceiling this voice has hit.
5. **Two readers want the consent line cut.** Priya: "'Published with each participant's consent.
   Names changed where he asked.' Real legal cover for fictional men. The satire pauses to prove it's
   nice. Cut it." She pairs it against "We do not improve the quotes," which she called excellent and
   immediately undercut. `recoveryStories.note` is an R4 item.
6. **The site has no quiz, and four of five participants say the quiz is what turned them.** Dylan
   arrived on "lol bro take this quiz," searched the navigation twice, and found a pledge form
   instead: "it asks me to commit before I've been diagnosed — backwards. I'd have taken the quiz in
   the first thirty seconds and I'd have taken it to prove I'd ace it, exactly like Chad." The Sniff
   Test lives in Got Soap?, so this is a cross-site seam question, not a CWAAA route — but it is the
   clearest funnel finding in three rounds of reads.
7. **Priya's hire verdict moved up and got specific.** "I'd want that person on concept + copy lead
   for a campaign, senior freelance rate, and I'd specifically brief them on institutional-voice
   work: packaging legalese, terms pages, warranty cards, the back of the can. They can write an
   organization as a character, which almost nobody can." Her Slack line: "someone made a fake
   women's coalition that fights body spray with a records committee and one of the case files is a
   genuinely moving story about a coal miner — the testimonials are AI mush, skip to
   /recovery-stories/billy-bob."
8. **Billy Bob is the site, per all three.** Maya: "the one line on this whole site that isn't funny
   at all, and it's the reason I'd trust the funny parts" (of "I sleep facing him again"). Dylan:
   "they let a man be right on the logic and still wrong on the outcome." Priya: "I read that page
   twice. Whoever wrote it is the person I'd hire." Maya's wince at the opening paragraph — a fear
   the site was laughing at a working-class man — resolved completely, and she named the two moves
   that resolved it: the reasoning is presented as correct, and the case worker concedes.
9. **The five index monologues read as one voice to Priya.** "Five testimonials, all with the
   identical funnel arc, each ending on a one-line win… that's a prompt producing variations, not
   five men." She named specific tells: Brayden's anaphora ladder ("Women notice. They notice your
   clothes…"), the mother coda, Chad's "Vanity got me in the door. Soap kept me there" antithesis,
   Marcus's four punchlines in five sentences, and Kaelthas's "the answer scared me sober." Maya and
   Dylan both read them as four distinct men, so this is a genuine two-to-one split, not a
   convergence — but Priya is the reader who judges whether it is hireable, and the tells she listed
   are checkable. An R4 revision target for the participant testimony, owner's call.
