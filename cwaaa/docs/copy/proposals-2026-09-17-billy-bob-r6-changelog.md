# Billy Bob, RC-064 — R6 change log (historical, not authority)

**This file is not copy authority and must not be read as canon or as current text.** It exists so a
human editor can audit what changed between R5 and R6. It necessarily quotes struck language — every
line the owner's 2026-09-17 review rejected — for comparison. A future writer or model should treat
`proposals-2026-09-17-billy-bob-r6.md` as the sole current authority for this record, and should not
draw language, structure, or tone from the "Old" column below. R5 (`proposals-2026-09-15-billy-bob-r5.md`)
remains the historical record of the R4→R5 pass and is likewise not current authority.

---

**Testimony, shower line.**
Old: "I shower tonight, I'm covered again tomorrow."
New: "What am I washing for tonight when I'll be covered again tomorrow?"
Reason: canon states he does not wash; the old line was declarative and contradicted it.

**Testimony, length and structure.**
Old: seven paragraphs, including a claim he could recite his wife's complaint "word for word," and a
closing "She thinks I don't listen. I listen. I just don't agree."
New: four short paragraphs ending on "It doesn't change what's waiting on me in the morning."
Reason: canon requires the excuse to read as self-serving disregard, not a considered position argued
at length; the cut ending also contradicted the reporting party's own statement that he has never
acted like he didn't hear her.

**Referral fields.**
Old: `Reported cleansing frequency — None reported`, `Non-working days — No change reported`,
`Fragrance substitution — Not reported`, `Household impact — Reported`, `Shared-surface impact —
Reported`, `Participant's awareness of concern — Reported as explicit and repeated`.
New: `Cleansing frequency — None`, `Cleansing on non-working days — None`, `Fragrance substitution —
None`, `Household impact — Withdrawal of physical contact`, `Shared-surface impact — Occupational
residue transferred to bedding`, `Awareness of concern — Confirmed`.
Reason: "reported" language records that information exists without stating it; canon requires the
total-refusal before-state to be stated as fact, not left ambiguous.

**Reporting party's statement, defense of the work.**
Old: "That is not a thing against him and it is not a thing against the work."
New: cut.
Reason: canon states his occupation is not the satirical target and needs no defense recited by a
character who would not say it.

**Reporting party's statement, lifetime implication.**
Old: "There is no day of the week I have ever known that man to get in a shower," alongside "I have
always minded it."
New: "Sunday makes no difference... I cannot remember the last time that man got into a shower,"
alongside "I mind it. I'm done pretending otherwise."
Reason: the old phrasing implied total refusal across their entire relationship, raising why she
married him or waited this long to refer him; the fix keeps total refusal in the documented
before-state only.

**Reporting party's statement, Sunday repetition.**
Old: a paragraph establishing Sunday behavior, followed by a separate paragraph restating "workday or
Sunday, it makes no difference."
New: merged into one paragraph.
Reason: the two sentences said the same thing.

**Reporting party's statement, physical-withdrawal paragraph.**
Old: "I couldn't get to the end of the counting," and a separate later "I cannot remember the last
time I wanted him to touch me" — two uses of the same retrospective-counting construction.
New: "I stopped reaching for him. I sleep on the edge of the bed with my back to him. I don't want
him touching me when he comes to bed like that."
Reason: the counting device was composed rather than spoken, and repeating it beside the Sunday fix's
"cannot remember" line created a visible authored echo; the fix states the withdrawal as a present
fact rather than a retrospective one.

**Chronology, "without prompting."**
Old: "Participant restated the reporting party's concern accurately and without prompting."
New: "accurately," with "and without prompting" removed.
Reason: the adjacent field note has the caseworker prompting him ("asked him to tell it back to me"),
so the claim was false on its face.

**Field note, the "it."**
Old: "Then he explained to me why it did not apply to him."
New: "Then he gave me the same reason again."
Reason: the first fix (naming "it" as "the recommendation") still had the caseworker narrating that
the recommendation did not apply, which validates his position in the institution's own voice. The
new line reports that he repeated himself, without adopting his claim; "He does not believe he should
have to" is kept as the caseworker's own judgment immediately after, with no restatement of the same
thought twice.

**Arc field.**
Old label: "What changed," value "He started showering after every shift."
New label: "What he does now," value "He showers after every shift."
Reason: the old label asked a question ("what changed him") the record is not permitted to answer;
the new label matches what the field actually reports.

**Photograph note.**
Old: "Photograph on file · not reproduced. Retained as received" (R5), later "Photograph — reproduced.
Taken by the case worker, with the participant's permission. Shipped 2026-09-16 as
`cwaaa/src/assets/billybob.png`..." (first R6 draft).
New: "Photograph reproduced with participant permission. Taken during initial home contact."
Reason: the asset path, ship date, and internal correction history are production metadata, not
archive content; they do not belong inside a fictional record a future implementation might render
verbatim. Production note, kept outside the record: `cwaaa/src/assets/billybob.png`, shipped
2026-09-16.

**Caseworker spelling.**
Old: "case worker" (two words) in the field note heading and the photograph note.
New: "caseworker" (one word) throughout this record. No prior owner ruling on this spelling was
found; this revision picks one word for internal consistency and can be reversed if the owner prefers
otherwise.

**External review date, case returned date, follow-up date, Office reference.**
Old: `[date TK]`, `[TK]`, `Follow-up — TK` rendered as the literal string "TK" (or "[date TK — see
note below]" in the first R6 draft) on a page real visitors can reach; an interim reference guess,
`OLC-2231`, that did not match any existing CWAAA/Office numbering convention.
New: March 11, 2026 (requested), April 9, 2026 (returned), April 23, 2026 (follow-up), reference
`26-1183`.
Reason: the owner decided to use mundane production values rather than render a visible placeholder
or a blank field, and ratified these specific digits on 2026-09-17. The reference now follows CWAAA's
own finding-numbering family (`26-01`, two-digit year, hyphen, sequence) at Office scale, rather than
the earlier opaque digits-letter guess. The interval's *content* remains the canon mystery; only its
*existence on the calendar* is now stated.
