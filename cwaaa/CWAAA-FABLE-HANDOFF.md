## 1. The assignment in one sentence

Build CWAAA as a credible fictional national advocacy nonprofit whose meticulous records, modest material culture, and unreasonably serious commitment to ordinary soap-and-water hygiene make the satire land—without ever turning the organization into a fake government agency, a generic charity template, or an extension of the glossy Got Soap? campaign.

## 2. North-star creative direction

CWAAA should feel like the small national nonprofit whose briefing packet lands on the right desks: competent enough to trust, specific enough to be funny, and materially human.

The emotional result is **collective relief**: someone finally wrote this down, put a seal on it, and made the expectation legible.

The central joke is not that CWAAA is incompetent or extreme. The joke is that a completely reasonable baseline—showering and using soap—has required the full apparatus of a patient, organized national coalition.

The three-system rule is absolute:

- **Got Soap? campaigns.**
- **CWAAA validates, advocates, organizes, and files.**
- **The Office of Lather Compliance assumes jurisdiction.**

This site is authored only by CWAAA. It may link to the other two systems, but it may not borrow their visual or verbal voices.

## 3. What is canon, what is build copy, and what remains open

### Locked canon and product decisions

- Full name: **Concerned Women Against Axe Abuse**.
- Initialism: **CWAAA**.
- Entity: a fictional advocacy nonprofit and national coalition; never a government body.
- Established in **2024**.
- It began with one book club's informal grievance log.
- Scale line: **“Two million concerned women. Chapters in all fifty states. One demand.”**
- Mission: make routine soap-and-water hygiene the expected baseline and document the social costs of substituting fragrance for washing.
- The organization is modest, competent, patient, credible, and real within the fiction.
- Seal: a soap bar combined with a barred aerosol can.
- Pledge program: **The Lather Pledge**.
- Form name: **Form CW-1 — Declaration of Intent to Lather**.
- Washcloth-ribbon program: **Tie One On For Suds**.
- Public findings use `FINDING YY-NN` labels.
- Recovery records use `RC-NNN` identifiers and protect participant names within the fiction.
- Recovery records are participant advocacy files, never police evidence, enforcement records, criminal dossiers, suspect boards, mugshots, or allegations.
- The Sniff Test stays on Got Soap? and is not a CWAAA interaction.
- The Lather Pledge appears on both Got Soap? and CWAAA, but CWAAA owns the declaration, filing, receipt, and email fulfillment.
- Both pledge presentations use the same contract, fields, Buttondown audience, and `SWORN` success semantic.
- The Office never receives, stores, files, enforces, or processes pledge data.
- The pledge relationship is exactly two emails: one immediate receipt and one separately delivered current issue after a configurable short delay. It is not an ongoing subscription or drip.
- CWAAA and the Office are legally separate fictional entities in private production canon.
- Their operational relationship must remain publicly unresolved.
- CWAAA may link to the Office through a sparse, neutral citation. It never calls the Office a partner, parent, supervisor, operator, division, coordinator, or public-facing layer.
- `1-800-GOT-SOAP` belongs to Got Soap? and must not be presented as ordinary CWAAA contact information.
- The canonical IVR is a protected transmedia exception and does not authorize regulatory or threatening CWAAA web copy.

## 4. Audience, problem, and desired behavior

### Primary audiences

- Millennial and Gen Z women who recognize the dating-culture truth behind the joke.
- Men willing to take a simple, affirming hygiene pledge.
- Social visitors arriving from the Got Soap? campaign.
- Creative-industry viewers evaluating the world-building, writing, interaction, and design craft.

### The problem this site solves

The Got Soap? campaign creates attention and desire. CWAAA gives that attention somewhere credible to go. It turns a punchline into an organized social expectation, a low-friction pledge, a set of shareable findings, recovery stories, and visible solidarity.

### Desired visitor journey

1. Recognize that the coalition takes an absurdly basic standard seriously.
2. Trust the organization because the satire is precise, not sloppy.
3. Read a finding or recovery record and see that change is possible.
4. Sign Form CW-1 or share the program.
5. Optionally follow a restrained link back to Got Soap? or outward to the Office without receiving an explanation of the larger institutional relationship.

## 5. Voice system

### Voice attributes

- Procedural but readable.
- Earnest without sentimentality.
- Firm without menace.
- Dry because the procedure is specific.
- Helpful without sounding like customer-support software.
- Warm, orderly, and quietly overprepared.

### Humor rules

The humor comes from the mismatch between meticulous civic procedure and the ordinary act of washing with soap. A perfectly maintained finding table, a formal declaration, or a carefully tied washcloth ribbon can deliver the joke. A participant's chosen behavior may also be ridiculous; CWAAA documents that behavior without making his identity or recovery a public-shaming exhibit.

The target is low-effort masculinity and fragrance substitution—not protected classes, medical barriers, disability, poverty, or a person's worth.

### CWAAA may say

- “The baseline is soap.”
- “Put it on the record.”
- “A reasonable expectation, clearly stated.”
- “Progress has been documented.”
- “Next action: continue.”
- “Two million concerned women. Chapters in all fifty states. One demand.”

### CWAAA must never say

- “We are monitoring you.”
- “Please remain available.”
- “Your access has been recorded.”
- “You are under investigation.”
- “The Office has your information.”
- Any containment notice, terminal ID, surveillance cue, prosecution language, diagnosis, threat, or regulatory command.
- Any statement resolving how CWAAA and the Office work together.

## 6. Visual system

### Design thesis

Use the visual language of letterhead, field reports, chapter packets, washcloth ribbons, stamped declarations, annotated findings, and carefully maintained participant advocacy files.

The paper should feel quiet and handled, not dirty or distressed. One or two tiny registration imperfections can imply physical production; heavy grunge makes the coalition look unserious.

### Color tokens

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| `paper-manila` | `#EFE6CF` | Primary paper stock and overall warmth |
| `letter-white` | `#FBFAF5` | Correspondence, form surfaces, clean reading fields |
| `ink-navy` | `#2F3E5C` | Primary text, rules, seal linework |
| `stamp-red` | `#A63D2F` | `SWORN`, required marks, sparing emphasis |
| `carbon-grey` | `#6D6A63` | Metadata, notes, duplicate-copy cues |
| `ribbon-cloth` | `#B94B45` | Washcloth ribbon only; not a generic CTA color |

Do not add unnecessary gradients. Do not translate “editorial brutalism” into concrete grey, black boxes, or fashionable neo-brutalist UI.

### Typography

- **PT Serif:** reports, letters, findings, form instructions, long reading.
- **Libre Franklin:** navigation, labels, chapter metadata, accessible controls.
- **Montserrat:** form numbers, dates, legal copy, document indexes.
- **Never use Oswald** except within a quoted Got Soap? campaign artifact.

Use document hierarchy, not landing-page hierarchy: issue number, title, short finding, supporting record, disposition.

### Identity assets

**Seal:** a competent nonprofit emblem combining a soap bar and a barred aerosol can. It may anchor the masthead, stamp declarations, and identify CWAAA publications. It must not resemble a federal seal, state seal, police badge, or Office emblem.

**Washcloth ribbon:** a visibly folded strip of terry cloth tied in the silhouette of an awareness ribbon. It should look tactile, affectionate, and handmade—not like a generic vector ribbon. Use it on the program page, pledge success, chapter materials, and event collateral.

### Photography and imagery

**SUPERSEDED 2026-09-15 by owner direction.** The still-life instruction below was wrong and is
struck. See `GRAPHICS-TO-MAKE.md` § CW-G04 and `docs/ui-system.md` §13 for live authority.

**No still life.** CWAAA does not use object-only photography anywhere on the site. Every image has a
person in it. The men in this campaign are synthetic — there is no likeness to protect, no one to
embarrass, and no consent to obtain. Substituting a folded washcloth or a stamped form for the man
being described is the single failure that flattens this satire: it makes the Coalition look like it
is apologizing for its own findings. It is not. It files them.

Participant photographs read as **submitted and retained as received** — the picture a man like this
already has of himself. Phone-camera light, ordinary composition, no retouching, no editorial grade,
no styling by CWAAA. The gap between what he chose to send and what the committee writes underneath
it is the entire joke.

Still avoid: smiling volunteer stock, staged boardroom diversity, and generic charity-hero framing.
The objection to those is that they are **generic and institutional**, never that they show people.

Paper, seal, ribbon, rules and stamps remain the typographic system. They are chrome around the
people; they never replace them.

## 7. Anti-template constitution

Do not build:

- a centered hero with a subhead and two pill buttons;
- a three-card “Our Impact” row;
- a generic Donate button;
- pastel wellness branding;
- a distressed conspiracy dossier;
- a fake federal masthead, flag, eagle, service-navigation grid, or `.gov` styling;
- a dashboard of animated charts;
- a SaaS feature grid;
- oversized campaign/thirst-trap typography across nonprofit prose;
- dark-mode surveillance aesthetics;
- randomized case-file generation;
- decorative stamps with no semantic purpose.

Each ordinary advocacy page needs a clear documentary purpose and an appropriate way to continue.
A Recovery Story may close on a concrete earned benefit; a proof record may close on its strongest
fact; an unindexed Office-derived fragment may close on unresolved implication. Do not attach a
CTA or direct quiz path merely to satisfy a page-wide formula. Preserve the ordinary index's
accidental pagination path into unindexed records.

## 9. Layout and interaction behavior

### Global shell

- Masthead resembles credible nonprofit letterhead: seal, full name, established date, and a compact chapter-scale line.
- Desktop composition uses a disciplined asymmetric grid with strong rules and generous reading space.
- Mobile preserves document order. Marginal notes move after the content they annotate.
- Avoid floating cards. Use attached sheets, ruled zones, file tabs, clipped annotations, and index strips where semantically appropriate.
- Footer behaves like a colophon/legal line, not a sitemap warehouse.

### Motion

CWAAA motion is administrative:

- a stamp settles;
- a file tab aligns;
- a copied link confirms;
- a ruled annotation appears in reading order.

No steam, smoke, cinematic parallax, cursor pursuit, scan lines, surveillance tracking, or ominous loaders.

Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reduced motion removes movement, not meaning.

### Responsive findings

On wide screens, findings may use ruled tables and marginal annotations. On narrow screens, recompose each row into a labeled record. Do not force a horizontal-scrolling dashboard.

### Record interaction

Record navigation must be keyboard-complete, predictable, and index-like. Opening a record reveals testimony, reform status, and next action. It never reveals a “charge,” “risk score,” or “evidence.”

## 10. Accessibility, privacy, and ethics

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA.
- Use semantic headings, tables, records, forms, and status announcements.
- Maintain comfortable body size and line length.
- Provide persistent form labels, adjacent error messages, visible focus, generous targets, and text equivalents for stamps.
- Never convey a state with red alone.
- Pledge success must be announced to assistive technology.
- Never send first name, email, consent value, or honeypot value to analytics.
- Do not use IP recognition, fingerprinting, personal profiles, or cross-device identification.
- Do not present fictional recovery records as real allegations.
- Do not imply affiliation with Axe/Unilever, MADD, a government body, or a public-health organization.

### FINDING 26-01

**Fragrance is not a cleansing event.**

**Observed condition:** Body spray is applied where soap and water were expected.

**Finding:** Scent can be added. Sweat, oil, and dirt must be removed.

**Recommended action:** Shower. Use soap. Apply fragrance afterward if desired.

**Disposition:** Clarified.

### FINDING 26-02

**A clean shirt does not reset the person wearing it.**

**Observed condition:** Fresh clothing is used to imply that all preceding hygiene steps occurred.

**Finding:** Laundry and bathing are complementary procedures. Neither completes the other by association.

**Recommended action:** Wash the person. Then dress the person.

**Disposition:** Sequence restored.

### FINDING 26-03

**“I don't smell it” is not an independent review.**

**Observed condition:** The subject serves as both source and sole evaluator of the relevant air.

**Finding:** Familiarity is not evidence of absence.

**Recommended action:** Do not litigate the atmosphere. Take the shower.

**Disposition:** Self-certification declined.

### FINDING 26-04

**The gym shower is part of the gym.**

**Observed condition:** Exercise concludes; hygiene is treated as an unrelated future project.

**Finding:** The final repetition is soap.

**Recommended action:** Build recovery time into the routine. Include water.

**Disposition:** Program guidance issued.

### Founding account

Concerned Women Against Axe Abuse began with one book club's informal grievance log.

The first entries were anecdotal. Then patterns appeared. Categories were added. Definitions were debated. Someone brought a binder. By 2024, the matter had outgrown the living room.

Today, CWAAA is a fictional national coalition with two million concerned women and chapters in all fifty states. Its work is simple: advocate for routine soap-and-water hygiene, publish findings, support practical reform, and give people a dignified way to put the commitment on the record.

### Mission

**CWAAA works to make routine soap-and-water hygiene the expected baseline and to document the social costs of substituting fragrance for washing.**

### What we do

- Publish plain-language findings.
- Maintain fictional participant advocacy records focused on progress.
- Receive and fulfill the Lather Pledge.
- Support Tie One On For Suds chapter activity.
- Provide chapter packets, finding handouts, pledge materials, and practical next actions.

### What we do not do

CWAAA does not investigate, diagnose, prosecute, monitor, or shame people. It does not publish real allegations. It is not a regulator or government body.

The standard is firm. The route to it gives a specific person a practical way to change without prosecution or public-shaming treatment.

### Chapters

CWAAA chapters are ordinary, carefully maintained spaces for finding handouts, pledge tables, washcloth ribbons, participant correspondence, and modest local events.

The final site should not include a fake interactive chapter locator unless real chapter content is separately authored. For the first build, present the national scale line and a **Request a chapter packet** control only if a real contact workflow is configured.

### Relationship to Got Soap?

Got Soap? is a separate fictional campaign that also presents the CWAAA-owned Lather Pledge. CWAAA authors the declaration, filing, receipt, and current issue; the campaign authors its own invitation and visual shell.

Conditional action: **Visit Got Soap? — external site**

### External reference

> **External reference:** Office of Lather Compliance. This link leaves CWAAA.

Do not add an explanation, preview, partnership label, jurisdiction, or service description.

### Satire disclosure

CWAAA is fictional satire within the Got Soap? project. It is not a real advocacy nonprofit, government agency, public-health organization, or chapter network. It is not affiliated with Axe, Unilever, MADD, or any similarly named organization. All findings and recovery records are fictional.

## 23. Component inventory

- `FindingDisposition`

- `DocumentFooter`

Names are suggestions; the behaviors and authorship boundaries are requirements.

## 24. Technical baseline

- Portable static-site implementation.
- Astro is preferred for compatibility with the existing project, but Fable may choose an equivalent stack if it preserves portability and Netlify deployment.
- Centralize all page copy in one content module or clearly structured content collection.
- Centralize `GOT_SOAP_SITE_URL` and `OFFICE_SITE_URL` as optional external-link configuration.
- Empty cross-site values render no controls.
- Self-host fonts where licensing permits.
- Use responsive images with explicit dimensions and modern formats.
- Use privacy-conscious analytics such as GoatCounter only for anonymous interaction events.
- No user accounts, backend personal profiles, IP recognition, or fingerprinting.

### Permitted analytics events

- finding viewed;
- record viewed;
- pledge started;
- validation failed, without field values;
- pledge submitted successfully;
- share selected;
- copy link selected;
- cross-site navigation selected.

Never record quiz answers, pledge contents, name, email, consent value, Office browser-local state, or recovery-story input.

## 25. Required states to design

- Default masthead and navigation.
- Mobile navigation.
- Finding list and responsive record transformation.
- Case-file index.
- Case-file detail.
- Form default, focus, valid, invalid, submitting, provider error, and `SWORN` success.
- Copy-link confirmation.
- Conditional external link present/absent.
- 404 missing-record page.
- Reduced-motion version of the stamp interaction.
- High-contrast and keyboard focus states.

## 26. Creative acceptance test

The result passes only if:

- CWAAA feels trustworthy enough to sign a pledge.
- The seriousness makes the joke sharper instead of flattening it.
- The site is visually and verbally distinct from Got Soap? and the Office.
- No visitor could mistake CWAAA for a regulator or government agency.
- No page looks like a generic nonprofit template.
- Findings feel maintained, not data-visualized for show.
- Recovery records present people as participants rather than suspects, preserve the fiction/name
  boundary, and document a specific, persuasive change.
- The seal looks nonprofit-specific, not governmental.
- The washcloth ribbon looks like cloth, not a generic awareness icon.
- The primary action is obvious without turning the page into a conversion funnel.
- Mobile preserves hierarchy and document meaning.
- The full experience is keyboard complete and WCAG 2.2 AA.
- The pledge visibly promises exactly two messages and nothing ongoing.
- The Office is never said to receive, file, store, or enforce the pledge.
- No dead external controls appear before URLs are configured.

## 27. Forbidden build outcomes

Reject the build if it introduces any of the following:

- “Donate,” fundraising metrics, volunteer headshots, or invented charity operations.
- Fake live chapter addresses or a fake locator.
- Government language, regulatory authority, badges, `.gov` conventions, or service-portal chrome.
- Police/evidence language for recovery records.
- Dark conspiracy styling, red string, classified stamps, or surveillance motifs.
- A newsletter signup that implies recurring delivery.
- More than two Form CW-1 messages.
- Office-authored form copy or Office data handling.
- Sniff Test questions or verdicts inside CWAAA.
- Got Soap? poster art used as CWAAA's core visual identity.
- A direct explanation of how CWAAA and the Office work together.
- A contact presentation of `1-800-GOT-SOAP` as CWAAA's phone number.
- Fabricated real-world affiliations.

## 28. Recommended build sequence for Fable

1. Establish the typography, paper, rule, seal, and ribbon system.
2. Build the global masthead, navigation, external-reference treatment, disclosure, and footer.
3. Build Home as the visual north star.
4. Build Findings and its responsive record transformation.
5. Build Recovery Records index and detail templates.
6. Build Tie One On For Suds.
7. Build Form CW-1 with all accessible and provider states.
8. Build About and the CWAAA-specific 404.
9. Add conditional Got Soap? and Office links only when real URLs are configured.
10. Verify content, authorship, responsive behavior, accessibility, pledge parity, finite email fulfillment, privacy, and visual distinctness.

## 29. Final instruction to the builder

Do not decorate the joke. Institutionalize it.

Every rule, stamp, tab, label, and line of copy must help CWAAA do one of four things: state the baseline, document the finding, protect the participant, or make the next action easier.

If an element does none of those things, remove it.
