person → a closing two-beat aphorism/imperative blade. Five different costumes, one sentence
architecture — so in sequence they blur. The fix is **form-level**: five distinct grammatical
attacks, each matched to its poster's world, and only ONE keeps the staccato telegraph (the
poster whose whole bit is being an announcement).

The attack map:

4. **redemption** — the witness account: before/after narrative time.
5. **thirst-announcement** — the official advisory: clipped telegraph (sole staccato survivor).

# R2 — Sol's bounded adversarial review (2026-07-29)

## Set-level ruling first

The diagnosis is correct: the live notes share a sentence engine, and the repair has to happen in
grammar rather than costume. The R1 set does not yet fulfill its own attack map. It says only the
announcement will retain the staccato, but Slots 2 and 3 preserve paired short buttons and Slot 4
becomes the most staccato paragraph in the set: seven sentences, with five clipped declaratives
after its one long setup. Distinct openers do not cure a repeated landing mechanism.

Machine floor before judgment: `npm run copy-gates` → **7/7**. That proves only the invariants;
the copy rulings below remain craft judgments against `CAMPAIGN-INTENT` §8, the live posters, and
the blind finding.

## Unit 1 — Confident Man: CHALLENGE, one deletion

The cumulative genre teardown works. The jaw/gaze/voiceover inventory is visible fragrance-ad
grammar, and `he showered first, and the fantasy held` preserves aspiration instead of asking him
to enjoy his own humiliation. The tile inventory is a specific close rather than another moral.

One sentence explains the sentence before it:

> "That's the entire product."

`Product` has no stable referent — soap, the fantasy, or the fake cologne — and the sentence pauses
to state the interpretation the reader has already made. That is the exact trust-the-reader cost
the pass is supposed to remove. Delete it. The long opener should hand directly to the tile line.

**Ruling:** challenge only that sentence; the remaining shape clears.

## Unit 2 — Soap-Smoldering: CLEAR, with one cross-set dependency

`Reports are consistent` risks institutional diction in isolation, but the opening-act simile and
the immediate group-chat source resolve the reports as gossip, not CWAAA evidence. The form carries
her distribution channel while `Be him by Saturday` keeps his aspirational exit open. The final
pair is clipped, but the full note has a corroborated-sighting lead rather than an advisory
telegraph; it does not duplicate Slot 5's whole performance.

**Ruling:** clear as written, provided Slot 3 relinquishes `group chat`. One distinctive social
object cannot do the differentiating work on two adjacent campaign pages.

## Unit 3 — Unholy: CHALLENGE, mechanism not premise

Sermon grammar is the right premise. This execution converts it into a visible writing exercise:
three consecutive `Blessed … for …` constructions, followed by the retained two-sentence button.
Calling the triple "liturgy" does not exempt it from the reader effect of a rule of three. The
form announces itself before the clean man arrives.

Two further mechanisms make the cost concrete:

1. `the ordinary man` turns ordinary men as a class into the downwind problem. That opens on
   generic funk and broad disgust, not the double standard or the reformed-man aspiration.
2. `Blessed is the group chat, for it remembers everything` repeats Slot 2's social object and
   gives it a generic memory aphorism instead of a new scene.

The baked `UNCOMMON. UNHOLY. Unreasonably hot.` rhyme is legal and potentially useful; it is not a
license to build three beatitudes around it. Keep at most one beatitude, make the clean man its
subject, let the congregation or third pew register the effect, and remove the group chat. Do not
preserve both short closing sentences merely because they were gold in the old architecture; the
round exists to change that architecture.

**Ruling:** challenge. The sermon register stays; the triple anaphora, `ordinary man`, duplicate
group chat, and inherited two-button landing do not.

## Unit 4 — Redemption: CHALLENGE, root brief not met

The pull-echo cut is correct and remains locked: the case note should not reopen with `He sinned.
He sprayed.` The confession/absolution pair and the altar call also remain useful material.

The proposed witness account does not actually move in narrative time. `There was a before. In the
before … Then, actual water.` labels a before/after frame, then delivers the same clipped sequence
the diagnosis identified. With seven sentences, this is the set's strongest surviving instance of
short declarative beat → short declarative beat → aphoristic close.

`the people next to him practiced breathing through their mouths and calling it love` introduces a
second problem: plural bystanders become romantic partners who knowingly rename endurance as love.
Nothing on the poster or in the live pull establishes that relationship, and the line shifts the
joke from his substitution behavior onto their complicity.

Rebuild this as the proposed form actually promises: one continuous before→after witness sentence,
with aerosol before the shower and confession/absolution after it; then, if needed, let `Some of you
should start tonight` be the sole short altar-call blade. Remove `calling it love` rather than
defending its warmth.

**Ruling:** challenge. The redundancy diagnosis clears; the replacement architecture does not.

## Unit 5 — Public Thirst Announcement: CLEAR

The mother echo advances rather than merely repeats the pull: the pull poses the meeting standard;
the note reports that he passed it and keeps the dinner reservation alive. `background check` is
immediately decoded by `your mother`, so it fixes the current risk-assessor abstraction without
borrowing CWAAA procedure. The clean man remains the aspirational outcome and `Wash accordingly`
keeps the action explicit.

This poster's baked performance is already a public announcement. It is the one place where the
telegraph is content, not a shared house rhythm.

**Ruling:** clear as written.
