# Got Soap? — Voice Bible

**Current voice authority for this campaign world.** Apply the campaign-register tests to campaign copy; judge CWAAA, participant, Office, and `/about` copy by their own register and surface job below. A generic campaign line is wrong, but a plain fact, sincere benefit, procedural sentence, or reality-sourced Stacey line may be exactly right. Do not soften the owner's premise in the name of safety. When a live creative choice remains unresolved, recommend a direction and let the owner decide.

## The one real line (the only limit)
Target **behavior, rationalization, ad tropes, entitlement, and the double standard** — the
Febreze-instead-of-showering logic, the man who "hasn't met soap since the Obama administration" but
is holding out for a 10. Do not make a person's body, race, class, disability, region, age, or
identity the joke merely for existing.

The human subject and the satirical target are not automatically the same thing. A likable or
sympathetic man may carry the joke while his chosen behavior or bad reasoning is what gets cut.

The reformed-hero exit stays open: the joke is always "you *could* be the poster," never "you're
disgusting and that's the end of it." He may be sincerely wrong, sincerely corrected, and still
recognizably himself afterward. That is what keeps it *clever* instead of cruel — and clever is the
flex.

## The dual-address engine (why it lands with everyone)
Campaign lines know whom they address; the best marquee lines can serve both audiences:
- **At him:** "got soap?" is an accusation wearing a smile. PSA-voice, aimed at the man viewing it. He laughs because it flatters what he could become (the Old Spice trick).
- **At her:** she's lived the deprivation — every dating app, every "you smell nice?" panic. The site hands her the mic and the locker-room wall. She's the distribution channel.
- **Both at once (the gold standard):** *"He washes. Daily. With soap."* flatters the reformed man AND makes her laugh in recognition.

## Voice dials
| Dial | Push toward | Away from |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Fragrance-ad drama + PSA sincerity + pop-culture edge | Explaining the joke, winking, "lol jk" |
| Attitude | Owns it, unbothered, a little dangerous | Hedging, "maybe," "kind of," apology |
| Reference | Dating apps, thirst-trap culture, revival-tent camp, 90s ad nostalgia | Generic "guys be like" |
| Targeting | Behavior / contradiction / rationalization / trope | A demographic or human being merely for existing |
| Length | As short as the surface permits; every sentence earns its place | Padding, qualifiers, mandatory punchlines |

## Surface logic — one voice system, different jobs

Do not force one satire rubric onto every page.

- **Attack:** expose the contradiction; a blade may be the right ending.
- **Conversion:** make the target want the improved state; reward may be the right ending.
- **Proof:** let the strongest fact carry the weight.
- **CWAAA institutional:** procedural sincerity is the joke.
- **Mystery / Office:** implication beats explanation.
- **Character:** the person speaks in their own cadence.
- **Reveal:** truth outranks campaign performance.

**The ending completes the job of the surface.** Sharpness is one tool, not the definition of a
successful ending.

A sincere line is allowed to remain sincere when it is doing strategic work. "My mom hugs me longer
now" can be stronger satire than another punchline because it proves the behavior change improved
human closeness without announcing the moral.

## Sound-like / doesn't-sound-like
**Sounds like her:**
- "This started on a dating app, where men who hadn't met soap since the Obama administration were holding out for a 10. So I made them an ad campaign."
- "'I usually shower' is not a hygiene routine. It's a confession."
- "We could smell you from the results page."
- "A body spray is a citrus arrangement on a condemned building."

**Does NOT (kill on sight):**
- "Because self-awareness is step one, and step two is soap." *(explains itself, no teeth)*
- "Smell nice and feel confident!" *(brand-neutral pablum)*
- Anything that would fit on a generic wellness landing page.

## The Stepford test (campaign-register test)
Run this on **campaign attack/persuasion copy**, not mechanically on every CWAAA record, participant
quote, Recovery Story, or Office fragment.

1. Could this line come from *any* brand? → rewrite.
2. Does it explain the joke instead of being it? → cut the explanation.
3. Is there a hedge word used as an apology (almost, maybe, kind of)? → delete it.
4. Does it know who this campaign line addresses — him, her, or both? → make it choose.
5. Would Stacey say it out loud, unapologetically? → if not, sharpen until yes.

For non-campaign surfaces, use the **surface-function test** instead:
- Conversion: does the reader want the better state?
- Proof: does the fact feel credible and specific?
- CWAAA: does the institution sound sincere and competent?
- Character: does this sound like the person rather than the copywriter?
- Office/mystery: does it create implication without explanation?

## Guardrails that protect the brand (not the timidity)
- Voice invariants still hold verbatim (Because… / Join the movement. / hashtag stack / funded-by gag / © line). Edge lives *inside* the template, not by breaking it.
- **Registers never blur:** campaign smolders; CWAAA files; participants sound like themselves; the
  Office processes; plain Stacey speaks from reality on `/about`. Vivian may write behind several of
  them, but Vivian's cadence does not become every speaker's cadence.
- CWAAA's comedy is deadpan bureaucracy, not smolder — it never flirts, and that restraint is *its* edge.
- Recovery Stories are **conversion + character surfaces**. They may end warm when the warmth proves
  the benefit. Earned reward is not the same thing as apologetic reassurance.
- Posters stay untouched.
- The reveal (`/about`) is where the fourth wall breaks and the real voice is loudest — that's the line
  a CD remembers, so it's the boldest, most personal copy on the site.
